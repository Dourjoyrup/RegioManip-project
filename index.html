Paper

Abstract

Contact-rich tool manipulation remains a last-mile challenge for embodied artificial intelligence and industrial robotics, because many real tasks require sustained physical interaction rather than simple pose reaching. Recent vision-language model (VLM) pipelines improve open-vocabulary grounding and instruction following, but their keypoint- or waypoint-centric outputs often commit to a single prediction and do not explicitly encode contact-frame stability, wrench limits, or perceptual uncertainty.

We present RegioManip, an AI-driven zero-shot framework that converts grounded tool regions into wrench-bounded action constraints for contact-rich manipulation. Given a single RGB-D observation and a natural-language instruction, RegioManip grounds each task-relevant region into grasp-type, contact-geometry, force-mode, and contact-face attributes, cross-checks them against geometry-only estimates, and prunes infeasible combinations through a physics-consistency lattice. The resulting ranked affordance hypotheses are lifted into SE(3) contact-frame hypotheses and executed with force and torque budgets derived from the grasp wrench space of the resolved contact. Confidence-adaptive strictness and hierarchical repair enable robust execution under perceptual uncertainty.

On a JAKA Zu3 across six industrial tool operations, RegioManip achieves 80.8% average open-world success, compared with 65.0% for GeoManip and 57.5% for MOKA, and reaches 90.8% success with a FoundationPose prior versus 86.7% for ToolManip.

Method

Three visual reasoning stages

01

Ground regions

Fuse RGB-D and language into candidate tool and target regions and a structured VLM prompt.

02

Check physics

Cross-check geometry and semantic assignments, then prune inconsistent grasp/contact/force triples.

03

Lift to SE(3)

Back-project clean regions, fit metric primitives, rank fixed-origin frames, and select an executable pose.
